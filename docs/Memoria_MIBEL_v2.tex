\begin{abstract}
% =====================================================================
\noindent
We build a residual-based surveillance system for the Day-Ahead
electricity spread between Spain and France in the Iberian
Electricity Market (MIBEL), 2019--2024 ($n = 52\,530$ hourly
observations), using only publicly available data. The contribution
is twofold. First, we deliver an operational alert cascade
(Verde/Ámbar/Naranja/Roja) that detects $83.3\%$ of a pre-specified
panel of 40 documented congestion events on the 2024 out-of-sample
test, with a Pareto-optimal calibration achieving identical coverage
at $4.3$ alerts/month and precision@72h of $0.212$. The mean-error
fundamentals model that drives the cascade does not statistically
beat AR(1) on point prediction at conventional levels
($p \in [0.06, 0.12]$ on Diebold--Mariano squared error). A quantile
companion specification with identical features
(\texttt{reg:quantileerror}, $\alpha = 0.95$) directly optimises tail
loss and outperforms AR(1) on the Giacomini--White conditional pinball
test ($p < 10^{-3}$). Second, we establish the empirical ceiling of
ex-ante prediction of the underlying congestion regime from open
hourly Day-Ahead features. Two hypotheses pre-registered before
modelling are falsified by walk-forward weekly evaluation: a
binary-state classifier with 33 physical features achieves
AUC-ROC $\leq 0.87$; a 3-state Gaussian Hidden Markov Model
underperforms ($0.60$) by a wide margin. The residual transition
noise is therefore stochastic rather than hidden-state separable;
further gains require data not publicly available at hourly DA
granularity (intraday flows, XBID transactions). The system is
positioned as a quantitative screening layer compliant with REMIT II
Article 15, where the four-week notification window from awareness
admits an alert lead time of $180$h ($\approx 7.5$ days).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

The Iberian Electricity Market (MIBEL) is coupled with the rest of
Europe through the Spain--France interconnection. When commercial
Available Transfer Capacity (ATC) is sufficient, the two markets
converge to a common price; when the interconnection saturates, the
markets decouple and a price differential (\emph{spread}) emerges
that reflects the physical congestion of the link
\citep{Newbery2016}. Market surveillance under REMIT II requires
identifying these events early enough that an analyst can produce a
documented notification within the four-week window stipulated in
Articles 15(1) and 15(2) of the Regulation \citep{ACER2024}.

This paper develops and evaluates a residual-based surveillance
pipeline for the ES--FR DA spread that combines a fundamentals model
(XGBoost) with an anomaly-detection cascade on the residuals, and
positions it explicitly within the empirical limits of what
hourly DA data can support. Two distinct contributions are reported.
First, an \emph{operational} contribution: an alert hierarchy that
matches an independent pre-specified event panel under transparent
Pareto-calibrated thresholds. Second, a \emph{methodological}
contribution: pre-registered hypotheses --- one on a single-step
two-state classifier (H1) and one on a Hidden Markov Model with three
latent states (H2) --- both empirically falsified by walk-forward
evaluation. The combination of operational utility and pre-registered
methodological limits is the central claim of the paper: the
proposed cascade does as well as the data allow, and the data do not
allow more.

The remainder is organised as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the data, the regulatory regimes,
and the empirical structure of the spread itself (zero-inflation,
transition asymmetry, conditional distribution).
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} introduces the feature set, the
two-model architecture, and the cascade.
Section~\ref{sec:econometrics} reports the econometric evaluation
(Diebold--Mariano, Giacomini--White, Kupiec--Christoffersen, LQRA,
cross-year robustness, feature importance).
Section~\ref{sec:operational} validates the cascade against the
40-event panel and presents the Pareto calibration.
Section~\ref{sec:limits} reports the pre-registered hypothesis tests
and establishes the empirical ceiling for ex-ante prediction with
open DA data.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes and lists data requirements
for a successor system.

\section{Data and empirical structure of the spread}\label{sec:data}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Data sources and licensing}

All inputs are public:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Day-Ahead prices ES and FR}: OMIE auction settlement
hourly prices, 2019-01-01 to 2024-12-31 (OMIE public API).
\item \textbf{Net Transfer Capacity ES$\leftrightarrow$FR}:
JAO SWE CCR explicit-auction NTC, hourly, from September 2022.
For 2019-2021 we use an implicit NTC proxy derived from OMIE
clearing flags; the dataset carries a binary
\texttt{ntc\_is\_observed} indicator so the model can discriminate
between observed and imputed periods.
\item \textbf{Gas (TTF Front Month, EUR/MWh)} and
\textbf{CO$_2$ (EU ETS EUA, EUR/t)}: daily Yahoo Finance close,
forward-filled to hourly. Real-time deployment would require a
licensed Euronext or ICE Endex feed; this caveat is part of the
operational positioning (Section~\ref{sec:operational}).
\item \textbf{Documented event panel}: 40 ES--FR congestion events
2019--2024 curated from CNMC bulletins, OMIE annual reports, REE
operational notes and RTE \emph{Bilan Électrique}, with
\texttt{date\_start}, \texttt{date\_end}, type
(MARKET / INFRASTRUCTURE / CLIMATIC / REGULATORY / LABOUR /
GEOPOLITIC) and magnitude (BAJA / MEDIA / ALTA / MUY\_ALTA).
\end{itemize}

The full dataset (52,530 hourly rows, 45 columns including all
derived features) is in
\texttt{data/processed/mibel\_dataset\_20190101\_20241231.parquet}.

\paragraph{Caveat on \texttt{yfinance}.} TTF and EUA from
\texttt{yfinance} are practical but not a primary regulatory source.
A serious operational deployment requires a subscription to
Euronext/EEX (TTF) or ICE Endex (EUA).

\subsection{Regulatory regimes}\label{sec:regimes}

We define three regulatory periods motivated by structural breaks in
the electricity market:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{pre\_crisis}: 2019-01-01 to 2021-12-31. Pre-pandemic
and pre-energy-crisis baseline.
\item \texttt{crisis\_y\_excepcion}: 2022-01-01 to 2023-12-31.
Aggregates the first half of 2022 (energy crisis without intervention)
and the Iberian Exception (Royal Decree-Law 10/2022, entered into
force 15 June 2022) until its termination on 31 December 2023.
\item \texttt{post\_excepcion}: 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31.
Post-intervention regime, used as the out-of-sample test for the
operational results.
\end{itemize}

Régime-specific calibration is justified empirically by the within-
regime spread dispersion: $p_{99}|s|$ ranges from $5.97$ (pre\_crisis)
to $34.63$ €/MWh (crisis\_y\_excepcion), a factor of nearly six.

\subsection{Empirical structure of the spread}\label{sec:dgp}

The spread $\mathrm{spread}_t = \mathrm{price\_es}_t -
\mathrm{price\_fr}_t$ is not a continuous heavy-tailed series. It is
a zero-inflated process with two physically distinct states:
\emph{coupled markets} where arbitrage forces prices together and
\emph{decoupled markets} where the interconnection is binding.
Table~\ref{tab:zero-inflation} reports the mass concentration.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Zero-inflation: distribution of $|\mathrm{spread}|$ across
the full 2019--2024 sample.}
\label{tab:zero-inflation}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Threshold} & \textbf{Hours} & \textbf{Fraction} \\
\midrule
$|s| = 0$ exact            & 50\,180 & 95.53\% \\
$|s| \leq 0.5$ €/MWh       & 50\,476 & 96.09\% \\
$|s| \leq 1$ €/MWh         & 50\,567 & 96.27\% \\
$|s| \leq 2$ €/MWh         & 50\,807 & 96.72\% \\
$|s| > 50$ €/MWh           &     124 & 0.24\% \\
$|s| > 100$ €/MWh          &       9 & 0.02\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The implication for the modelling design is direct: a regression on
$\mathrm{spread}_t$ with mean-squared-error loss devotes most of its
capacity to predicting zero, an outcome that a trivial constant
predictor already matches 95.5\% of the time. The interesting
behaviour is in the 3.91\% of hours where $|s| > 0.5$, and the
target of the surveillance system is precisely this tail.

\paragraph{Binary state and transitions.} We define
$S_t = \mathbb{1}\{|\mathrm{spread}_t| > 0.5\,\text{€/MWh}\}$. The
threshold is justified by the small mass between $0$ and $0.5$
(0.6 percentage points, consistent with auction-clearing tick noise).
The empirical $2 \times 2$ transition matrix is

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & $S_{t+1} = 0$ & $S_{t+1} = 1$ \\
\midrule
$S_t = 0$ & \textbf{0.9851} & 0.0149 \\
$S_t = 1$ & 0.3656 & \textbf{0.6344} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Both states are persistent but asymmetrically so. The decoupled
state has typical run length of 2--3 hours (median 2, p95 8, max 17)
while the coupled state runs for tens of hours (median 23, mean 67,
max 798). The decoupling rate also varies $8.7\times$ by hour of
day, from $0.96\%$ at 22h to $8.41\%$ at 15h (solar peak ibérico).
Conditional on $S_t = 1$, the magnitude distribution is itself
heavy-tailed (median $|s|$ given decoupled $= 5.9$ €/MWh, p95 $56$,
max $122$) and asymmetric: $75\%$ of decoupled hours have
$\mathrm{spread} > 0$ (ES $>$ FR).

\paragraph{Implication for architecture.} These properties motivate
the two-model architecture of Section~\ref{sec:methodology}: a
mean-error model whose residuals drive the operational cascade, and
a quantile companion model that targets the upper-tail magnitude
directly. Neither is a direct two-stage hurdle model
(Section~\ref{sec:limits} explains why such a hurdle, when tested,
fails to deliver the expected gains given the available features).

\subsection{Data audit and residual diagnostics}\label{sec:eda}

A retrospective audit of the dataset
(\texttt{scripts/eda\_audit.py}, full report in
\texttt{results/eda\_audit\_report.txt}) confirms the data-quality
preconditions:

\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{spread\_da}_t = \mathrm{price\_es}_t -
\mathrm{price\_fr}_t$ holds to numerical precision (no sign
convention error).
\item No duplicate timestamps; gaps in the hourly index correspond
to documented DST transitions.
\item The 9 hours with $|s| > 100$ €/MWh map to verified event
timestamps (28 August 2022 morning during the FR nuclear-availability
trough; 1 May 2023 negative-price episode in ES).
\item Negative DA prices in \texttt{price\_es} ($0.37\%$) and
\texttt{price\_fr} ($0.47\%$) are real market outcomes during
oversupply episodes, not data errors.
\end{itemize}

Diagnostic checks on the residuals of the canonical mean-error model
(see Section~\ref{sec:methodology}) reveal three properties relevant
for interpretation. (i) On a single 2019--2023 training the
in-sample residual lag-1 autocorrelation is only $-0.08$; the
apparent $+0.61$ in the concatenated walk-forward residuals is an
artefact of fold-boundary discontinuities, not model
mis-specification. We verified this empirically: an AR(6) correction
fitted on training residuals \emph{deteriorates} the test RMSE
(3.41 $\to$ 3.58 €/MWh, Diebold--Mariano $p = 0.0001$ in favour of
the uncorrected baseline), confirming that within-fold structure is
adequately captured. (ii) Residual skewness $= 8.2$ and excess
kurtosis $= 154$ confirm the heavy-tailed non-Gaussian nature of the
residual distribution; this is the formal explanation for why
parametric upper bounds based on residual normality under-cover
catastrophically (Section~\ref{sec:econometrics}). (iii) The Engle
ARCH-LM test on 24 lags rejects homoscedasticity
($p < 10^{-3}$), which is the empirical justification for
régime-specific threshold calibration in the cascade.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Feature engineering}\label{sec:features}

The feature set \texttt{FEATURES\_A\_CLEAN} (26 variables, see
listing in \texttt{scripts/train\_model\_v3.py}) is constructed
strictly with respect to the information available at the moment of
DA auction clearing. It contains:

\begin{itemize}
\item Spread lags at 1, 2, 3, 6, 12, 24, 48 and 168 hours
(\texttt{spread\_da\_lag*h}). Within-day lags (1--12 h) are
acceptable under the surveillance framing because they are observed
ex-post; this is consistent with how AR(1) is benchmarked against
the model.
\item Rolling mean and rolling standard deviation of the spread at
24 h and 168 h horizons, computed with \texttt{shift(1)} to avoid
leakage of the target.
\item Energy fundamentals: TTF gas, CO$_2$ EUA, spark spread, clean
spark spread, with TTF lags at 24 h and 168 h.
\item NTC ES$\to$FR, NTC FR$\to$ES, and a binary
\texttt{ntc\_is\_observed} flag (1 from September 2022, 0 before).
\item Calendar: hour, day-of-week, month, weekend, night, peak.
\end{itemize}

An earlier set including ATC-derived features
(\texttt{atc\_congestion}, \texttt{atc\_fatigue}) was discarded
because in the 2019--August 2022 sub-period these features are
defined as a direct transformation of the spread itself
(\texttt{jao\_downloader.py:148--157}), introducing leakage. The
\texttt{FEATURES\_A\_CLEAN} suffix flags their exclusion.

\subsection{Two-model architecture}\label{sec:architecture}

Two XGBoost specifications share the same feature set and training
window (2019--2023 expanding) but differ in the loss function and
the use of their output:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Model A v3} (\textbf{mean-error},
\texttt{objective=reg:squarederror}): standard XGBoost on the spread.
Its residuals drive the operational alert cascade.
The residuals are centred near zero, which is what the cascade
requires.
\item \textbf{Model A v4} (\textbf{quantile},
\texttt{objective=reg:quantileerror}, $\alpha = 0.95$): identical
feature set, only the loss changes. The predictions target the
conditional 95-quantile of the spread and are evaluated
econometrically against AR(1) and AR(24) on the matching pinball
loss. Its predictions are not used for the cascade (they are biased
upward by construction).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Anomaly detection cascade}\label{sec:cascade}

The cascade processes the residuals of Model A v3 in three parallel
detectors and combines them into four alert levels.

\paragraph{Rolling $z$-score.} For each hour $t$ the residual is
normalised as
$z_t = (r_t - \mu_{t-168 : t-1}) / \sigma_{t-168 : t-1}$ with a
mandatory \texttt{shift(1)} that excludes the contemporaneous
residual. A numerical floor at $\sigma \geq 0.1\,\sigma_{\text{global}}$
and a symmetric $\pm 10$ clip on $z_t$ prevent artefactual
amplification in low-volatility periods.

\paragraph{Isolation Forest.} An IsolationForest is trained
per-regime on the first 50\% chronological half of the regime and
applied to the full regime; the score is normalised to $[0,1]$
within each regime.

\paragraph{Bilateral CUSUM.} A bilateral cumulative-sum statistic
on standardised residuals with reference parameter $k = 0.5$ and
decision threshold $h = 5$ counts consecutive hours of CUSUM
activity.

\paragraph{Alert cascade.} The four-level classification, identical
to the original PDF specification, is

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Verde}: $|z| < p_{90}^{\text{regime}}$
\emph{and} IF$_{\text{score}} < p_{95}^{\text{global}}$.
\item \textbf{Ámbar}: $p_{90} \leq |z| < p_{95}$
\emph{or} IF $\geq p_{95}$.
\item \textbf{Naranja}: $|z| \geq p_{95}$
\emph{and} (IF $\geq p_{95}$ \emph{or} CUSUM persistent $\geq 1$h).
\item \textbf{Roja}: $|z| \geq p_{99}$ \emph{and}
IF $\geq p_{95}$ \emph{and} CUSUM persistent $\geq 3$h.
\end{itemize}

The percentile thresholds $p_{90}, p_{95}, p_{99}$ are computed
\emph{only} on the first 50\% temporal half of each regime, then
applied to the rest of the regime. This avoids look-ahead. The
estimated regime-specific values for the test period
(\texttt{results/regime\_thresholds\_v3.json}) are
$p_{99}^{\text{post\_excepcion}} = 6.12$,
$p_{99}^{\text{crisis\_y\_excepcion}} = 5.74$.

\section{Econometric evaluation}\label{sec:econometrics}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Walk-forward and competitive benchmarks}\label{sec:bench}

Training is performed on an expanding-window scheme: data
$<$ 2024-01-01 is used to train, the year 2024 is the
out-of-sample test ($n = 8\,783$). We compare against five
benchmarks:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{AR(1)} and \textbf{AR(24)} via
\texttt{statsmodels.AutoReg}, rolling 1-step-ahead.
\item \textbf{LASSO} with \texttt{LassoCV(cv=5)} on the standardised
\texttt{FEATURES\_A\_CLEAN}.
\item \textbf{M0 (persistence)}: $\hat{s}_t = s_{t-24}$.
\item \textbf{M0b (zero)}: $\hat{s}_t = 0$.
\end{itemize}

Statistical comparison uses the Diebold--Mariano test
\citep{Diebold1995} on squared-error loss with Newey--West HAC
variance (Andrews 1991 lag rule). 95\% CIs on RMSE are computed
with circular block-bootstrap (1000 replicates, 24h blocks,
sensitivity at 168h).

Model A v3 achieves lower RMSE than all five benchmarks but with DM
significance \emph{only} against the naive benchmarks
($p < 10^{-3}$); against AR(1), AR(24) and LASSO the positive DM
stat does not reach 5\% ($p \in [0.06, 0.12]$). This is not a
defect of the model under the surveillance use case but a metric
mismatch: squared-error loss weighs equally a 1 €/MWh error in the
$95\%$ of hours where the spread is zero and a 5 €/MWh error in the
tail. The relevant testing instrument for the tail is the pinball
loss; the next subsection addresses it directly. $R^2 = 0.539$ is
below the $> 0.85$ typical of single zonal-price forecasts
\citep{Weron2014} but in line with the lower side of the range
reported for bilateral spreads \citep{Bunn2016}; the bilateral
differencing cancels most of the common-factor variance that drives
high $R^2$ in single-price models.

\subsection{Régime-specific models}\label{sec:regime-specific}

A natural alternative to a single pooled model is one regression per
regulatory regime. We train régime-specific Models A on the same

\subsection{Quantile companion and the Giacomini--White pinball
test}\label{sec:gw-pinball}

The mean-error specification predicts the conditional expectation;
pinball loss at $q = 0.95$ targets the conditional 95-quantile.
These are distinct estimands, and a model trained on the first is
expected to underperform on the second. We verify this directly:
on the 2024 test, Model A v3 has mean pinball loss
$0.50$ at $q = 0.95$ versus $0.33$ for AR(1); the Giacomini--White
conditional pinball test \citep{Giacomini2006} rejects equality in
favour of AR(1) with statistic $+15.07$ ($p = 5 \times 10^{-4}$).

A quantile companion --- Model A v4, same XGBoost,
\texttt{objective=reg:quantileerror}, $\alpha = 0.95$, on identical
\texttt{FEATURES\_A\_CLEAN} --- directly optimises the relevant
loss. On the 2024 test:

quantile specifications are therefore complementary: the mean-error
model feeds the cascade (residuals centred at zero); the quantile
model provides the formal econometric evidence on the upper-tail
question.

\subsection{Calibration: Kupiec and Christoffersen}\label{sec:calibration}

Beyond mean pinball loss, we evaluate the calibration of the
$q = 0.95$ quantile predictions \citep{NowotarskiWeron2018}. On the
2024 test the empirical exceedance rate of the v4-quantile
prediction is $5.58\%$ against a nominal $5\%$ (490 exceedances
versus 439 expected). The Kupiec (1995) unconditional-coverage LR
test rejects equality at the 5\% level
($\mathrm{LR}_{\text{uc}} = 5.98$, $p = 0.014$), but the deviation
is small in absolute magnitude ($+0.58$ p.p.). By contrast, a
parametric upper $q = 0.95$ derived from AR(1) under Gaussian
residual assumption yields $1.55\%$ empirical exceedance
($\mathrm{LR}_{\text{uc}} = 298.3$, $p < 10^{-3}$), a catastrophic
under-coverage by a factor of three driven by the heavy-tailed
nature of the residuals
(skewness $8.2$, excess kurtosis $154$; see Section~\ref{sec:eda})
that the normality assumption fails to capture.

Christoffersen's (1998) LR independence test rejects independence
of exceedances in both cases ($\mathrm{LR}_{\text{ind}} = 764.6$ for
v4-quantile, $190.9$ for AR(1)-parametric). Exceedances cluster in
time, consistent with the persistence of congestion episodes
documented in Section~\ref{sec:dgp}. The joint
conditional-coverage test is therefore rejected for both
specifications. The reading is methodological: v4-quantile is
approximately but not exactly calibrated and inherits the clustering
that any single-state model would inherit on this series; the
parametric Gaussian alternative does not even achieve approximate
coverage.

\subsection{LASSO-Regularized Quantile Regression Averaging
(LQRA)}\label{sec:lqra}

Following \citet{UniejewskiWeron2021} we combine multiple point
forecasts into a probabilistic forecast via $L_1$-regularized
quantile regression. The five inputs are v3-mean (XGBoost MSE),
AR(1), AR(24), LASSO and a decomposed forecast (XGBoost on
\texttt{price\_es} minus XGBoost on \texttt{price\_fr}). For each
target quantile $q$, a separate \texttt{QuantileRegressor} with
$L_1$ penalty is fit on the first half of the 2024 test and
evaluated on the second half; the regularization strength is
selected by minimizing pinball loss on a 20\% inner hold-out of the
training half.

inputs to LQRA are conservative point forecasts of the conditional
mean and the ensemble inherits that conservatism, a known limitation
of pinball-minimizing ensembles without explicit calibration
constraints.

\section{Empirical limits of ex-ante prediction}\label{sec:limits}
% =====================================================================

The cascade is forensic by design: the median 180h lag means the
system flags events one week after onset, well within REMIT II's
four-week notification window but far from real-time alerting. We
report here the experiments and pre-registered hypothesis tests that
characterise the empirical ceiling for ex-ante prediction with open
hourly DA data, and the implications for any successor system.

\subsection{Lag decomposition (9-variant ablation)}\label{sec:lag-ablation}

To attribute the 180h median lag we run an ablation across nine
cascade variants (\texttt{scripts/ablate\_lag\_components.py},
\texttt{results/ablation\_lag\_components.csv}). Key findings:
(i) removing the IsolationForest and CUSUM gates (V2, only
$|z| \geq p_{99}$) yields a 185h median lag against 180h baseline,
five hours; the AND cascade is \emph{not} the dominant source of
the Orange-level lag. (ii) The full Red-alert cascade
($|z| \geq p_{99} \wedge \mathrm{IF} \geq p_{95} \wedge
\mathrm{CUSUM} \geq 3$h) carries 777h median lag (recall 0.54);
at the Red level the AND cascade \emph{is} the bottleneck.
(iii) Reducing the rolling window to 24h is strictly worse
(1281h median lag, recall 0.25): the régime-specific $p_{99}$
threshold inflates from $6.12$ to $9.11$ on a short-window
distribution with heavier sample-variance tails. (iv) An EWMA
estimator ($\lambda_\mu = 0.94, \lambda_{\sigma^2} = 0.97$) with
régime-specific thresholds calibrated at $p_{95}$ of $|z_{\text{EWMA}}|$
on the training half and persistence $\geq 2$h achieves 144.5h
median lag with preserved recall 0.833 at the cost of a 40\% higher
false-positive rate (24.8 vs 17.8 alerts/month). This configuration
(V4b\_p2) is the recommended operational Pareto point for
deployments prioritising lead time over alert precision.

\subsection{Extensions tested without improvement}\label{sec:extensions}

Three methodologically natural extensions were implemented and
rejected on empirical grounds.

\emph{(i) Day-ahead renewable forecasts as features.} We integrated
the four publicly available D-1 renewable forecasts (ES wind and
solar from ESIOS 541, 542; FR wind and solar from RTE OpenData
\texttt{production\_forecast}), $30$ features instead of $26$.
Although the four new features appear in the top 12 of the v3-mean
permutation importance, they do not improve RMSE. The DM test on
squared-error loss between v3-mean with and without renewables
yields stat $= -3.83$ ($p = 0.0001$) in favour of the specification
without renewables, mean differential $-2.11$. Pinball loss at
$q = 0.95$ is unchanged. The DA auction already internalises the
forecasts at the moment of price discovery; the lagged exogenous
forecast at hourly resolution adds no detectable predictive signal
beyond the spread's own lags. Full results in
\texttt{results/renewables\_ablation.csv}.

\emph{(ii) Decomposed forecasting.} Following the strategy of
\citet{Lago2021} we trained two separate XGBoost models targeting
\texttt{price\_es} and \texttt{price\_fr} and combined them as
$\hat{p}_{\text{spread}} = \hat{p}_{\text{ES}} -
\hat{p}_{\text{FR}}$. Empirically the result is worse: RMSE
$3.77$ vs $3.41$ for direct, DM stat $+2.84$
($p = 0.0045$) in favour of direct; pinball at $q = 0.95$
$0.93$ vs $0.44$. The component models each carry
substantial residual variance ($\mathrm{RMSE}_{\text{ES}} = 3.99$,
$\mathrm{RMSE}_{\text{FR}} = 5.47$); subtraction does not cancel
errors enough to beat a model trained directly on the lower-variance
target.

\emph{(iii) Nuclear availability and demand forecasts.} We added
three further structural features --- French aggregated nuclear
availability (RTE \texttt{eco2mix} realised generation lagged 24h
to avoid leakage), Spanish day-ahead demand forecast (ESIOS 460) and
French day-ahead demand forecast (RTE \texttt{prevision\_j1}) ---
and re-trained both v3-mean and v4-quantile on the $29$- and
$33$-feature variants. None improves the baseline. For v3-mean,
RMSE deteriorates from $3.42$ to $3.79$ €/MWh (BASE+ALL,
DM in favour of BASE, $p < 10^{-3}$). For v4-quantile, pinball
loss at $q = 0.95$ deteriorates from $0.232$ to $0.265$
(GW $p \leq 10^{-3}$ in favour of BASE).
Full results in \texttt{results/v5\_features\_comparison.csv};
merged dataset in \texttt{data/processed/mibel\_dataset\_20190101\_20241231\_v5.parquet}.

The cumulative empirical reading is that the $26$-feature
\texttt{FEATURES\_A\_CLEAN} baseline sits at the informational
frontier achievable with publicly available day-ahead data at hourly
resolution. The two pre-registered hypothesis tests reported next
(H1, H2) further establish this ceiling formally on the binary
classification of the congestion regime.

\subsection{H1 (pre-registered): binary classifier with rich features}\label{sec:h1}

We pre-register, before running the experiment:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{H1}: a logistic-regression classifier with physical features
on $S_t = \mathbb{1}\{|\mathrm{spread}_t| > 0.5\,\text{€/MWh}\}$
achieves AUC-ROC $> 0.95$ \emph{and} AUC-PR $> 0.60$ in walk-forward
weekly evaluation over 2024.
\end{quote}

The features are six cyclical time variables, ES and FR national
holidays, $|\mathrm{NTC}|$ ES$\to$FR and FR$\to$ES, the
\texttt{ntc\_is\_observed} flag, state lags at 24h and 168h, the
current run-length of the state at $t-1$, and one-hot regime
dummies; rich variant adds the seven raw v5 features
(renewable and demand forecasts, FR nuclear availability) and eight
physically motivated derived features (penetration ratios,
asymmetries, demand log-ratio, nuclear-to-demand ratio).

\subsection{H2 (pre-registered): Gaussian HMM with 3 states}\label{sec:h2}

We pre-register, before running the experiment:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{H2}: a Gaussian Hidden Markov Model with 3 latent states
({coupled, tensioned, decoupled}) with emissions conditional on the
continuous physical features achieves AUC-ROC $> 0.92$ \emph{and}
AUC-PR $> 0.45$ in walk-forward weekly evaluation over 2024,
where the prediction is the smoothed posterior
$P(\text{state}_t = \text{decoupled} \mid x_{1:t-1})$.
\end{quote}

The implementation is \texttt{hmmlearn.GaussianHMM(n\_components=3,
covariance\_type='full')} on 23 continuous physical features
(cyclical time, NTC, renewable forecasts, demand forecasts, nuclear
availability, and 8 derived penetration and asymmetry ratios);
discrete and categorical variables are excluded as they violate the
Gaussian emission assumption. Decoupled state identification is
post-hoc by ranking the three states on the fraction of empirical
$S_t = 1$ assigned to them by Viterbi on training data.

H2 is falsified by a wide margin. The HMM correctly identifies the
decoupled state in 52/52 weeks post-hoc, so the failure is in
predictive capacity rather than in state mapping. Several weeks
trigger EM convergence warnings, consistent with the joint
distribution of the features not being well approximated by a
mixture of three multivariate Gaussians (bounded sin/cos features,
categorical flags, and the JAO/implicit jump in NTC violate the
Gaussian emission assumption).

\subsection{Empirical ceiling and implications}\label{sec:ceiling}

The combination of falsified H1 (best AUC-ROC $\leq 0.87$ with
LightGBM and 33 features) and falsified H2 (HMM AUC-ROC $0.60$,
worse than LightGBM by $0.27$) establishes the following empirical
claim:

\begin{quote}
The ex-ante prediction of the ES--FR binary congestion regime
$S_t = \mathbb{1}\{|\mathrm{spread}_t| > 0.5\}$ from publicly
available hourly Day-Ahead features has an empirical ceiling
of AUC-ROC $\approx 0.87$. The residual transition noise is
stochastic rather than hidden-state separable, and persistence
of the observed state is the dominant predictor by a wide margin
over any physical driver. The v1 cascade reported in
Sections~\ref{sec:operational}--\ref{sec:pareto} achieves
performance commensurate with this ceiling.
\end{quote}

Three implications follow. First, further marginal gains on
\emph{ex-ante} prediction with these data are unlikely; the
operational system is at the empirical frontier. Second, real
improvement requires data not available at hourly DA granularity:
intraday OMIE forecasts and clearing, REE/RTE physical flows
(rather than published NTC), and XBID transactional data --- all of
which are partly public but require additional integration work or
operator agreements. Third, the surveillance positioning of the
system is unchanged: the cascade is a screening filter compatible
with REMIT II Article 15(1)/15(2), where the four-week notification
clock starts at awareness (not at occurrence) and §457
(\citealp{ACER2024}, p.~122) explicitly admits notification of past
breaches with documented justification of the delay.

\subsection{REMIT II positioning under the empirical
limits}\label{sec:remit-positioning}

The 180h median lag of the Orange-level cascade is compliant with
REMIT II by construction:

\begin{quote}
\textit{``without further delay and in any event no later than four
weeks from the day on which that person becomes aware of the
suspicious event''}---ACER Guidance on REMIT, 6.1st Edition,
§431 p.~118 and §432 p.~118 (citing Articles 15(1) and 15(2)).
\end{quote}

A 180h ($\approx 7.5$ days) lag from documented event onset to
first alert falls comfortably within the four-week notification
window. §457 (p.~122) explicitly admits notification of past
breaches provided the delay is documented and justifiable, and
\S464 (p.~123) on Article 15(3) requires PPATs and PPETs to
maintain \emph{``effective arrangements, systems and procedures
to identify potential breaches of Article 3, 4 or 5''} ---
which the Verde/Ámbar/Naranja/Roja cascade provides. ACER expects
automated alerts to undergo human screening before notification
(\citealp{ACER2024}, footnote 182, p.~122); the cascade is the
structured filter that meets this expectation. Article 4
(publication of inside information) is \emph{not} covered ---
consistent with \citet{ACER2024} §438 p.~120 requiring an active
approach for Article 4 monitoring rather than treating it as a
side product of Article 3/5 surveillance. The residual gap
relative to a Tier-3 ACER surveillance tool is the granularity of
the data (aggregate day-ahead prices rather than transactional
REMIT reports), not the alert latency.

\section{Conclusions and next steps}\label{sec:conclusions}
% =====================================================================

The paper delivers two complementary contributions on the
ES--FR Day-Ahead spread surveillance problem.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Operational}: an alert cascade
(Verde/Ámbar/Naranja/Roja) calibrated on régime-specific
percentiles, validated on a pre-specified panel of 40 documented
events with recall $0.833$ and a Pareto-optimal calibration at
$4.3$ alerts/month with precision@72h of $0.212$. The cascade is
compliant with REMIT II Articles 15(1)/15(2) and §457, positioned
as the structured human-screening filter expected by ACER.

\item \textbf{Methodological}: pre-registered hypotheses H1
(binary classifier with rich physical features) and H2 (Gaussian
HMM with 3 latent states) both falsified empirically by
walk-forward weekly evaluation; an upper empirical ceiling of
AUC-ROC $\approx 0.87$ for ex-ante binary prediction of the
congestion regime from publicly available hourly DA data. The
two-model architecture (mean-error for cascade, quantile companion
for tail econometrics) achieves what these data allow: a
Giacomini--White conditional rejection of equal predictive ability
versus AR(1) at $q = 0.95$ ($p < 10^{-3}$) on the 2024 test, and
approximate but not exact Kupiec calibration of the upper $5\%$
exceedance rate ($5.58\%$ empirical vs $5\%$ nominal,
$p = 0.014$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Data requirements for a successor system.}
A genuine improvement on the empirical ceiling requires inputs
that the current DA-only feature set cannot reach:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Intraday OMIE} sessions and clearing (partially
public via OMIE API; integration not trivial).
\item \textbf{Physical flows} on the ES--FR interconnection from
REE and RTE operational feeds, rather than the NTC values
published by JAO.
\item \textbf{REMIT transactional reports} at the order-by-order
level (XBID for the European intraday continuous market) ---
required for a Tier-3 ACER surveillance tool, not available with
open-data access.
\item \textbf{Sub-hourly granularity} (15-minute resolution) for
the MIBEL market post-2025, which will require migration of the
entire pipeline.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pareto-elbow recommendation.} For deployments under the
current data scope we recommend the elbow operating point of
Section~\ref{sec:pareto}
($z_{p_{99}}$ régime-specific, $\mathrm{IF}_{p_{90}}$ OOS,
$\mathrm{CUSUM} \geq 1$h) over the default cascade configuration:
identical recall, five-fold lower alert volume, and 43\% higher
precision@72h. For deployments prioritising lead time over
precision, the V4b\_p2 EWMA configuration of
Section~\ref{sec:lag-ablation} reaches a 144.5h median lag at the
cost of a 40\% higher false-positive rate.
